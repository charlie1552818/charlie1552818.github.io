CASE STUDY / SOFT & MORPHOLOGICAL ROBOTICS

SpiRob
morphology as computation.

A soft-robotics research direction exploring logarithmic-spiral/tendon mechanisms, mechanically assisted grasping and the co-design of morphology, sensing and control.

MORPHOLOGYSpiral / tendon
ACTUATIONCable-driven
TARGETAdaptive grasping
APPROACHCo-design
01 Idea02 Research chain03 System04 Principle
01 / IDEA

Do not ask control to compensate for every bad mechanical decision.

The project treats structure as part of the controller. Geometry, passive deformation and bistable/spiral mechanisms can shape the reachable set and contact behavior before software closes the loop.

02 / RESEARCH CHAIN

From morphology to experiment.

03 / SYSTEM

Co-designed subsystems.

A

Mechanical intelligence

Use geometry and elastic/bistable behavior to create preferred wrapping and capture modes.

B

Tendon actuation

Route cable forces so coarse positioning and tightening can be separated rather than overloaded onto one degree of freedom.

C

Perception

Use local vision/range sensing to estimate target pose and trigger grasp-state transitions.

D

Control layer

Coordinate morphology and actuation with an event-driven controller instead of forcing continuous precision everywhere.

04 / PRINCIPLE

The design rule.

A strong robotic system can move complexity from software into morphology when the physical mechanism naturally funnels the system toward useful contact states.
